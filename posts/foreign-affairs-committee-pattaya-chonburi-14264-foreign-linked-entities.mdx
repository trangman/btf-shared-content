# A Second House Committee Has Now Gone to Pattaya, and Chonburi's Own Agencies Put 14,264 Foreign-Linked Companies and More Than 7,000 Land Cases on the Table

<KeyTakeaways>

- **The provincial count**: the Chonburi Provincial Commerce Office reported 14,264 registered entities carrying a foreign investor or shareholder, a registry total, not a nominee finding.[^1][^2]
- **The risk sample**: of a first-phase review of 70 entities, the office rated 39 high-risk and 31 low-risk; 16 gave no clarification.[^1][^2]

## Who Was in the Room, and What Did Chonburi's Agencies Report?

Eleven agencies sent officials alongside Governor Naris Niramaivong and Mayor Poramet Ngampichet: Chonburi Province, Pattaya City, Bang Lamung District, Commerce, Employment, the Land Office, Immigration, Tourist Police, the DSI's Area 2 office, Provincial Police and Area Revenue Office 3.[^1][^2]

The Commerce Office, a provincial arm of the [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th), reported 14,264 entities carrying a foreign shareholder, a registry figure rather than a finding of wrongdoing.[^1][^2] Bang Lamung reported around 350 land holdings inside foreign-linked entities, plus more than 7,000 further cases.[^1][^2] Stated next steps are visits to boat-tour operators, an inspection of Walking Street, and an integrated approach linking records on individuals, companies, shareholders, financial transactions and land.[^1][^2]

## Is Today's 14,264 the Same as Last Week's 14,000?

No. Better-than-Freehold™ covered the [earlier House Tourism Committee hearing](/resources/house-tourism-committee-nominee-property-tourist-cities) on 13 September, which heard that roughly 14,000 Pattaya entities fit a police screening profile.[^3] Today's 14,264 is a separate measure, a whole-province registry count of companies that simply carry a foreign shareholder.

No source reconciles the two, and the closeness in size is coincidence. One is a city's suspected nominee profile as police built it. The other is a province's entire foreign-linked population as the registrar holds it, genuine joint ventures included. A foreign shareholder is lawful, and Chonburi treats the 14,264 as a group needing monitoring, not as offenders.

## What Does the 70-Entity Risk Sample Show?

The first-phase review covered 70 target entities. Fifty-four provided clarification and sixteen did not. The office rated 39 high-risk and 31 low-risk, the first published instance of officials sorting foreign-linked companies by risk rather than counting them.[^1][^2]

A first sample is chosen where suspicion already sits, so the 39 cannot be read across the registry. The sixteen non-responders were recorded too, which means silence in front of a commerce office now carries a mark of its own.

## What This Means for Owners and Buyers

An owner already inside a high-risk property-owning company in Chonburi saw no change in their legal position on 18 September. The province is now linking registry, land, immigration and financial transaction records, the [Anti-Money Laundering Office](https://www.amlo.go.th)'s own territory, so the window for an unexamined structure is closing. Divestment takes eight to sixteen weeks. Penalties have not changed: [Foreign Business Act](/resources/foreign-business-act-thailand) section 36 reaches the Thai nominee and the foreign beneficiary, up to three years and/or THB 100,000 to 1,000,000, plus cessation of the shareholding, daily fines of THB 10,000 to 50,000, and forced land disposal by the [Land Department](https://www.dol.go.th).

A data match is built to catch one pattern, a Thai name standing between a foreign buyer and the land with no genuine investment behind it. The compliant route does not produce that pattern.
