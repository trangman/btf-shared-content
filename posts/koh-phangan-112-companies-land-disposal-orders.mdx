## What Did the Island-Wide Register Sweep Find?

**Officials at the [Land Department](https://www.dol.go.th) checked all 1,832 land-holding companies on Koh Pha Ngan** and found 112 with foreign shareholding above the Land Code limit.[^1][^2] Not a raid on a tip. The whole register.

The 112 split in two: 104 identified fresh through inspection, 8 already subject to court rulings.[^1] Together they hold 124 plots, roughly 86.86 rai.[^1][^2] The earlier 14 August figure of 104 companies sits inside today's 112: that was the inspection cohort, never the full count.

Every one of the 112 sits at one of three stages: 8 ordered to sell, 28 pending a decision, 76 being compiled at the branch land office.[^1][^2] Nothing has been dropped.

For scale, Land Department director Pornpoj Penpart said in May 2026 that foreigners legally own only six per cent of land on the island.[^4]

## How Does a Governor's Disposal Order Actually Work?

**This runs through the Land Code's limit on foreign shareholding, not a [Foreign Business Act](/resources/foreign-business-act-thailand) prosecution.** A provincial governor administers it directly, once a [Department of Business Development](https://www.dbd.go.th) register filed with the [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th) fails the test.

An FBA case needs a prosecutor and a court. A governor's order just needs a failed register, so it moves faster and reaches the asset directly, with no case against any named person required. It runs alongside, not through, the source-of-funds checks [AMLO](https://www.amlo.go.th) applies to high-risk sectors.[^3]

## What Would Change Under the Proposed Forfeiture Amendment?

Today, a forced disposal costs the holder the land but returns the sale proceeds. A Land Code amendment under study would replace that with forfeiture to the State, returning nothing. Unenacted; direction, not deadline, but it changes what waiting costs.

## What Does This Mean for Existing Owners on Koh Pha Ngan?

Anyone already running an [at-risk property-owning company](/resources/nominee-company-risks-thailand) on the island should read these numbers as a countdown, not a curiosity. The exposure now sits with the land itself, on a timetable the owner does not set; a disposal order is a forced sale, not a considered exit.

A planned divestment runs eight to sixteen weeks, short against a queue already holding 76 companies.
