## Who Is Somchat Techathaworncharoen?

**A Phuket MP for the opposition People's Party, not Pheu Thai, known in Thai coverage as "Lawyer Kaeng."** Bangkok Post reports the DSI was preparing to summon him over transactions tied to several companies and a law office.[^1] No charge has been filed. A similar complaint arose earlier this year over another People's Party MP.[^1]

## What Do the Two Company Figures Mean?

**Two figures are circulating, and they measure different things.** Bangkok Post reports Somchat "linked as a shareholder and authorised director to more than 28 companies," and separately "suspected of being linked to more than 30 firms."[^1] Thai coverage adds a value: over THB 100 million in land and condos.[^2] The 28 is a role count. The 30-plus is a suspicion count. Neither adds to the other.

## What Have the Party and Parliament Said?

**The People's Party welcomes scrutiny, but not a name released ahead of the process.** MP Karunpol Thiensuwan said: "What concerns us is that we learned from the media that the DSI has not issued a summons and has not found any wrongdoing, yet the name of our MP was released as if he had already committed an offence."[^1][^4] The House Police Affairs Committee, chaired by Bhumjaithai MP Watcharapong Khuwijitsuwan, said on 19 August it would call DSI officials to explain.[^3]

## Why Does This Matter Beyond the Politics?

**This is not a comment on any party; it is about the pattern.** Non-trading companies sharing recurring Thai names, built to hold land and condominiums, are exactly the shape [DBD](https://www.dbd.go.th) screening exists to surface. Political prominence changes nothing in that test. Phuket, Surat Thani and Prachuap Khiri Khan are the fastest-growing markets and the provinces under heaviest [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th) enforcement, no coincidence, and the reported reach into a law office matters too, since the adviser sits inside the pool, not outside it.
