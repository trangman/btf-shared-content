# Nominee Inspections Reach Koh Tao: Twelve Tourism Companies Checked, and a Dive Operator Lost Its Licence the Same Day

## Thailand's Tourism Regulator Shows a Faster Way to Shut a Nominee-Linked Business

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Small scale, deliberate mechanism**[^1]: 12 tourism companies with foreign shareholders inspected on Koh Tao on 17 August 2026; 2 in breach, 9 compliant, 1 temporarily closed.
- **Licence pulled same day**[^1]: a tour operator with one Thai director out of two failed the majority-Thai-director rule under the Tourism Business and Guide Act B.E. 2551; its tour licence was revoked immediately.
- **Gatekeeper warning extended**[^2]: DBD Director-General Poonpong Naiyanapakorn cautioned consulting firms that register companies and advise foreign investors.
- **Better-than-Freehold™ takeaway**: an operating business layered on top of a property-owning company adds a licensing risk a compliant lease structure never carries.

</KeyTakeaways>

## What Happened on Koh Tao on 17 August 2026?

<QuickAnswer>

**Quick Answer:** On 17 August 2026, the Department of Business Development and four partner agencies inspected twelve foreign-shareholder tourism companies on Koh Tao, Koh Phangan District, Surat Thani. Two were in breach; one had its tour licence revoked the same day, and nine passed on shareholding, director composition and work permits.[^1]

## Which Companies Failed?

Neither breach needed a nominee charge to prove.[^1] The first was a tour company also offering diving instruction, with one Thai director out of two, short of the Tourism Business and Guide Act B.E. 2551 rule requiring a Thai majority.[^1] Its licence was revoked that day. It was also running an unlicensed hostel, and an unpermitted diving instructor went to Immigration.[^2] Three infractions. One afternoon.

The second operated a branch tour business without a licence; the Department of Tourism opened legal action.[^1] Complaints go to the [Department of Business Development](https://www.dbd.go.th) or the 1570 call centre.

## Why a Licence Beats a Nominee Charge

That detail outweighs the headcount. On Koh Phangan and Koh Samui, the instrument was nominee prosecution built on [AMLO](https://www.amlo.go.th) evidence: land seizures, forced disposal by the Land Department and dissolution by the courts. On Koh Tao it was administrative, resting on a director ratio that already sat in the company's own filing, no bank trail needed, and it suits Thailand's ongoing [FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org) evaluations better than a slow prosecution.

The underlying prohibition is still the [Foreign Business Act](/resources/foreign-business-act-thailand): a foreigner may not control a restricted business through a Thai front. Anyone who has read the risks in a [nominee company structure](/resources/nominee-company-risks-thailand) will recognise why tourism took the shorter route.

## What This Means for Owners

Anyone holding property through an at-risk property-owning company that also trades under a sector licence, hotel, tour, diving, restaurant or hostel, should read the licence as the softer target. Revocation cuts the trading income servicing the property, no nominee charge needed. A trading business on top of a property-holding structure multiplies exposure. Poonpong also warned consulting firms that register companies and advise investors, echoing the [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th)'s 2026 messaging.[^2] That places the adviser who drew up the director list inside the [gatekeeper liability](/resources/gatekeeper-liability-definition-thailand) frame closing around formation agents this year.

Nine of twelve survived intact: correct shareholding and director composition are auditable in advance.[^3] Divestment from an at-risk structure typically runs eight to sixteen weeks.
