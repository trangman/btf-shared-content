# Commerce and Interior Form a Permanent Nominee Team, and Their Shared Database Traces Shareholder Changes Backwards

## Thailand Turns Nominee Enforcement Into Permanent Machinery

<KeyTakeaways>

- **The new body**: Commerce and Interior agreed on 4 September 2026 to a permanent joint team against nominee structures, ending the campaign-by-campaign approach.[^1]
- **The database**: a shared "company-person-land" database, refreshed monthly, cross-checks registrations, shareholder changes, related persons and land holdings.[^1][^2]
- **The retrospective reach**: officials say a changed shareholder record does not close the file; the team traces history backwards.[^1]
- **Better-than-Freehold™ takeaway**: a compliant path never depends on the named Thai shareholder, so a database cross-check finds nothing in a Better-than-Freehold™ structure.

</KeyTakeaways>

## What Did Commerce and Interior Agree on 4 September?

<QuickAnswer>

**Quick Answer:** On 4 September 2026, Commerce Minister Suphajee Suthumpun and Deputy Interior Minister Pol Maj Gen Polpeera Suwannachawi agreed a permanent joint working team against nominee structures, combining the Departments of Business Development, Provincial Administration and Lands around a shared database that traces ownership history, not only current filings.[^1][^2]

## How Does the Company-Person-Land Database Work?

**Three departments now read from one file.** Business Development holds the company register. Provincial Administration holds identity records, and Lands holds the title deeds. Linked and refreshed monthly, the database lets an officer follow one company from its shareholders to the land it holds, and back again.[^1] That link between the corporate register and the land register is what gives the team something a raid never had: a standing view that outlasts the news cycle.

## Why Does the Retrospective Reach Matter Most?

**This should worry anyone who swapped a Thai shareholder some time ago and assumed the file closed with it.** Officials said a shareholder change would not defeat the review.[^1] The team traces the record backwards regardless. A swap made months ago is not a closed chapter. It's a data point waiting to be read. Cosmetic remediation doesn't erase the old record. Another swap simply adds another entry to trace.

## Who Else Is Exposed Besides the Named Owner?

**The officials named accounting firms, law firms and complicit state officials alongside the beneficiaries.**[^2] Exposure reaches whoever built the structure, not only whoever signs the register. That is [gatekeeper liability](/resources/gatekeeper-liability-definition-thailand) at work. The advisers who set these structures up are now the least likely people to defend them.

## How Does This Fit With Earlier 2026 Enforcement Measures?

**This is a new layer, not a repeat.** The 1 August [Department of Business Development](https://www.dbd.go.th) order requiring bank statements is a filing check at registration, taken under the [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th). A separate Interior Ministry tip-off channel, reported in late August, is a reporting route, not a database. This team sits above both. It checks old filings against the land register over time. That is the point. Enforcement is layering, not resetting.

One piece stays unenacted: an amendment that would make nominee conduct a money-laundering predicate offence, stalled since December, tightening what the [Anti-Money Laundering Office (AMLO)](https://www.amlo.go.th) already expects gatekeepers to report.

## What This Means for Owners and Buyers

**Nothing here changes what already applies. It only makes the file harder to close.**

- **Already holding through a high-risk property-owning company**: a recent shareholder change reads as a record, not a resolution. The response is documented [divestment from the at-risk structure](/resources/nominee-company-risks-thailand), eight to sixteen weeks, expected to reduce to under four as Better-than-Freehold™ grows, not a fresh registration.
- **Still weighing a structure**: a compliant path never depends on the named Thai shareholder, so it's unaffected by shareholder-to-land cross-checks.

Existing law already reaches a confirmed finding. The [Foreign Business Act](/resources/foreign-business-act-thailand) section 36 reaches the nominee and beneficiary alike, up to three years and THB 100,000 to 1,000,000, with court-ordered cessation of the shareholding; the [Land Department](https://www.dol.go.th) separately forces disposal of the land.
