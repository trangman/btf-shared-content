## How Did a Cemetery Become a Company Examination?

An inspection of an unlicensed cemetery in Bang Khla district, Chachoengsao, traced the land to Gan Chabad Co., Ltd.[^1][^2] Interior is coordinating with the [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th), the DSI and the [Anti-Money Laundering Office](https://www.amlo.go.th) on whether the company and linked firms were lawfully established.[^1]

Three shareholders were born in Israel and hold Thai citizenship, which is precisely what makes the examination legally difficult.[^1][^2] The DSI has issued subpoenas; no charge has followed.[^2] One outlet alone reports links to at least 25 other companies, a scale nothing else corroborates.[^1]

## What Happened in Phuket?

Phuket's governor inspected Chabad House Patong on 15 September under an Interior Ministry order.[^8] A municipal order dated 1 September had already found construction departing from approved plans, stopped use of the non-compliant areas and allowed 30 days to rectify, under the Building Control Act.[^4]

On 20 September customs officers temporarily seized the shop goods pending import documentation, and the provincial land office attended on land ownership and acquisition.[^6] Officials separately reported goods lacking the Thai-language labels required by law, a Food and Drug Administration matter, separate from the customs check.[^6] Bangkok Post reports five agencies ordered to examine two affiliated companies, including whether unlawful shareholding exists and whether those shareholders overlap with the cemetery company.[^5]

A THB 261.58 million total has been reported for two Choeng Thale companies, called combined assets by one outlet and a stated business value by another, and neither is registered capital held at the [Department of Business Development](https://www.dbd.go.th).[^3][^4]

## What Did the Minister Add on 22 September?

The Deputy Interior Minister set out the national picture on 22 September: eight or nine sites under examination, Phuket permit irregularities carrying 30 days to regularise or face demolition, unlabelled goods being prepared for seizure, and checks in Krabi and Bangkok.[^7]

At Phra Athit Road in Bangkok, no underground construction was found, contradicting earlier reports.[^7] And the figure of nearly THB 100 million is not a finding: MPs gave the minister an estimate for land bought in the three southern border provinces, and he said he intends to establish who owns it.[^7]

## Why Thai Citizenship Does Not Settle the Question

Section 36 of the [Foreign Business Act](/resources/foreign-business-act-thailand) asks whether a Thai person holds shares for the benefit of a foreigner. It does not ask where the shareholder was born. A naturalised Thai citizen is a Thai national in law, so citizenship answers a question the statute never poses. That is why such an examination is slow.

Where a nominee structure is established, section 36 reaches nominee and foreign beneficiary alike: up to three years' imprisonment, a fine, cessation of the shareholding and forced disposal of the land through the [Department of Lands](https://www.dol.go.th). No such finding exists here.

## What Owners Should Take From the Sequence

What matters here is what opens the file. None of this began with a land complaint; it began with a cemetery licence, a building permit and a customs form. Once several agencies attend together, the shareholder register is read beside the permit file, and a [high-risk property-owning company](/resources/nominee-company-risks-thailand) has two exposures at once. Divestment runs eight to sixteen weeks, and is best begun before an inspection brings several agencies to the door.
