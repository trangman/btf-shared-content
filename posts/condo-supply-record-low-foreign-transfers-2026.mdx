## How Weak Is the New-Launch Pipeline?

Bangkok Post puts 2026 launches at 15,000 to 17,000 units, a 20-year low; Greater Bangkok's Q2 figure, 2,332 units against 7,169 in Q1, shows why.[^1] Weak demand, elevated mortgage rejection rates amid tight [Bank of Thailand](https://www.bot.or.th) lending conditions, and developer caution recur throughout.[^1][^2] None names foreign scarcity as the constraint.

## Why Are Foreign Transfers Falling?

REIC's recorded Q1 figure shows foreign condo transfers down 17.3% to 3,241 units, value down 17.9%.[^3] K-Research's 20% full-year forecast has not happened yet.[^4]

The nationality split tells the real story. Chinese buyers, the largest group, pulled back hard: 906 units, down 38.8%.[^3] Russians did the opposite; units rose 33% to 383, value rose 68.7%, a smaller cohort buying pricier stock.[^3] None of it is enforcement.

## What This Means for Existing At-Risk Property-Owning Companies

An owner holding land through an at-risk property-owning company, the kind [DBD](https://www.dbd.go.th) audits now scrutinise, should read this as a timing warning. Land sits outside the 49% quota, but thinner buyer depth still lengthens a sale.

Divestment planned now, typically eight to sixteen weeks and expected to shrink to under four weeks as Better-than-Freehold™ grows, sits inside a market that still has buyers moving. Waiting for enforcement means selling into a weaker one.

## Impact on Foreign Property Buyers

A buyer weighing a condominium against a house faces two constraints: the [49% foreign quota](/resources/can-foreigners-buy-property-thailand-legal-guide), and a 0.01% fee stimulus, registered at the [Land Department](https://www.dol.go.th), for Thai nationals only. Foreigners pay the standard 2% fee.

A registered instrument set over a house carries neither constraint, provided the structure is genuinely compliant, not a Thai-shareholder company standing in for a foreign buyer.
