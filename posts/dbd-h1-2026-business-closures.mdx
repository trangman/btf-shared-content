# Company Dissolutions Rose 12.5% in the First Half of 2026, and the Capital Behind Them More Than Tripled

## The Companies Winding Up in Thailand Are Getting Much Larger

According to [Department of Business Development](https://www.dbd.go.th) figures reported by the Bangkok Post on 3 August, 7,024 businesses filed for dissolution between January and June 2026, up 12.5 per cent year on year.[^1] The registered capital attached to them reached nearly THB 99 billion, more than three times the prior-year total.[^1][^2] Property owners should read the second number, not the first.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Volume rose modestly**: 7,024 dissolutions in the first half of 2026, against 6,244 a year earlier.[^1][^3]
- **Capital rose sharply**: nearly THB 99 billion wound up, an increase of roughly 224 per cent.[^1][^2]
- **Real estate accounted for 400 closures**: carrying THB 4.21 billion, with construction, electrical installation and advertising also among the heaviest sectors.[^1][^2]
- **Better-than-Freehold™ takeaway**: dissolving the company that holds your property does not resolve how the property was held.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why the Capital Figure Matters More Than the Count

A 12.5 per cent rise in closures is unremarkable. A 224 per cent rise in the capital behind them is not.

The gap describes what kind of company is closing. Last year's dissolutions were dominated by small businesses carrying little capital. In 2026, well-capitalised companies joined them, which is why the capital total more than tripled while the headcount barely moved.[^1][^2] The [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th) data does not break the figures down by shareholder nationality.

That matters for foreign owners. A property-holding company is a capitalised entity by construction: it exists to hold an asset, not to trade. Companies of that shape sit in the group that grew.

## Where Property Sits in the Data

Real estate accounted for 400 dissolutions carrying THB 4.21 billion.[^1][^2] That is under six per cent of the total, so property is not leading this wave.

Be precise about what the data is not. These are dissolution filings, not nominee findings, and nothing in them identifies which companies held property through structures that would not survive scrutiny. Thailand's nominee enforcement campaign and this rise in closures are running at the same time. The figures do not establish that one produced the other.

## What Winding Up a Land-Holding Company Involves

Closing a company that holds land is not the same as selling the land. That is where owners often find the difference.

The asset does not dissolve with the entity. It has to go somewhere: a transfer recorded at the [Department of Lands](https://www.dol.go.th), a price, a tax position, a buyer. If the shareholding was built around Thai shareholders who did not fund their shares, dissolution does not close that question. It surfaces it, with paperwork attached. Exposure under the [Foreign Business Act](/resources/foreign-business-act-thailand) attaches to how the company was held, not to whether it still exists.

For anyone already in a nominee structure, winding up is an exit from the company, not from its history. Our guide to [nominee company risks in Thailand](/resources/nominee-company-risks-thailand) sets out where that exposure sits.

For anyone weighing divestment, the effect is liquidity. More structures unwinding means more assets reaching the market as a company sale, which is slower and narrower than a clean transfer. The [Bank of Thailand](https://www.bot.or.th) reports subdued domestic demand alongside it. Our [Thailand property market analysis](/resources/thailand-property-market-analysis) covers where that leaves pricing.
