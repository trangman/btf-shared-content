## How Did Officials Narrow 12,906 Companies to 59?

**The screening ran before the raid, not after it.** Every one of the island's 12,906 registered companies was reviewed; 8,254 had foreign shareholders; 875 showed nominee characteristics; 59 were selected, holding 37 plots across 31 rai worth an estimated THB 1.2 billion.[^1][^2] Around 300 personnel executed the warrants, the [Department of Business Development](https://www.dbd.go.th) among the agencies taking part, across five company groups; 14 arrested so far.[^1][^2]

## Why Does the Share-Transfer Sale Matter?

**Four German-linked companies show the mechanism plainly.** Investigators found luxury hillside villas, their construction permits suspected of being obtained improperly, sold by transferring company shares rather than the land itself, avoiding transfer tax and corporate income tax on the proceeds.[^1]

It is not the clean exit it looks on paper. A compliant buyer pays the standard 2% transfer fee. The reduced 0.01% rate is for Thai nationals only, not foreign buyers. Selling the company hides that liability, exactly what investigators listed as evidence of nominee intent.

## What Happened on Koh Phangan the Day Before?

**A separate operation ran on Friday 14 August, with its own figures.** Deputy Interior Ministers Polapee Suwunchwee and Worasit Liengprasit inspected five luxury hotels, none holding the required licences.[^3] Officials found 104 companies where foreigners held more shares than Thais, owning 124 plots including a ten-rai sea-view property, the [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th)'s DBD tracing ownership.[^3] Khaosod reports a 30-day deadline for remedial action followed, without specifying what it covers.[^4]

## What Does This Mean for Existing Owners?

**Six phases now sit behind Saturday's raid.** The campaign has examined 238 companies and 272 plots covering 184 rai, valued at roughly THB 2.8 billion, with 178 arrest warrants issued.[^1]

An owner holding through a high-risk property-owning company on Samui or Phangan carries that exposure whether or not their name has come up yet. Section 37 of the [Foreign Business Act](/resources/foreign-business-act-thailand) reaches the Thai nominee and foreign beneficiary alike: up to three years, THB 100,000 to 1,000,000, plus dissolution. The [Land Department](https://www.dol.go.th) can force disposal; the courts can order dissolution separately. A planned [divestment](/resources/nominee-company-risks-thailand) still runs eight to sixteen weeks. A raid does not shorten that.
