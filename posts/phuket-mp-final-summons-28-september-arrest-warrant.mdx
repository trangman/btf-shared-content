## The Mechanism the Minister Described

**Section 24 is a powers provision, not an arrest power itself.** It lets an official summon any person, witness or accused.[^4] Section 41 makes refusal an offence punishable by up to one year of imprisonment, a fine of up to THB 20,000, or both.[^4] Arrest after two ignored summonses is a Criminal Procedure Code rule, not this Act's; the Minister's route reads as a section 41 complaint.

## How Many Summonses Is This?

The Minister called this the third summons, discounting one never delivered.[^1] Our own tally already reached three; both counts are consistent, they simply measure differently.[^5] He denies any nominee role, saying not a fraction of a square millimetre of Thai land passed into foreign hands through his businesses.[^2] He remains a witness.

## What This Means for Owners

**Compulsion is the story, not the MP.** Someone accused of nothing can still be made to attend and answer, and refusing is itself an offence. That matters inside a high-risk property-owning company, where the question asked is the one that usually breaks a nominee structure: who provided the money. An [orderly divestment](/resources/nominee-company-risks-thailand) runs eight to sixteen weeks. A compliant structure under the [Foreign Business Act](https://www.boi.go.th/upload/Foreign%20Business%20Act_5dd766122ff27.pdf) answers that question first, and the [DBD](https://www.dbd.go.th) screen does not wait.
