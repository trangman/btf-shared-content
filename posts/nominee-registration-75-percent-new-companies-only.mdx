# The Commerce Minister Says the 75% Fall in Nominee Companies Counts New Registrations Only, Not the 119,297 Already on the Register

## Thailand's Commerce Ministry Separates a Falling Headline From a Growing Enforcement Pool

<KeyTakeaways>

- **A qualified 75%**: 141 new nominee-risk registrations from 1 to 20 August 2026, against 561 in the same period of 2025, a fall Suphajee Suthumpun herself capped at "nearly 75%" and applied to new filings only.[^1]
- **The pool the number leaves out**: 119,297 companies with foreign shareholding between 0.01% and 49.99% sat in the ministry's screening category as at 21 June 2026, out of 1,002,685 entities on the entire register.[^2]
- **Where enforcement is actually pointed**: 17,556 of those companies have been referred to the Land Department for property-holding checks, alongside 14,800 to the Revenue Department for tax audit.[^2]
- **Better-than-Freehold™ takeaway**: a structure with no foreign shareholder never enters the screening category in the first place, so a falling or rising registration count changes nothing about it.

</KeyTakeaways>

## What Did the Minister Actually Say on 30 August?

<QuickAnswer>

**Quick Answer:** On 30 August 2026, Commerce Minister Suphajee Suthumpun clarified that the reported 75% fall in nominee-risk companies covers newly registered entities only: 141 recorded from 1 to 20 August 2026, against 561 a year earlier. She stated explicitly that this does not mean nominee structures across Thailand fell by 75%.[^1]

## What Does the 75% Figure Actually Measure?

**The comparison covers three weeks, not the whole of Thailand.** Between 1 and 20 August 2026, the DBD recorded 141 newly registered nominee-risk companies against 561 a year earlier, a fall of 420 that Suphajee rounded to "nearly 75%."[^1]

Real, but a narrow slice; Thairath's 24 August write-up of the 90-day briefing had run the same fall without her qualifier.[^3]

A second series exists: from 1 January to 31 May the group fell 65.22%, 2,102 to 731.[^2] Different period, different base, different percentage.

## What Counts as a "Nominee-Risk" Company, and What Does the Accumulated Total Show?

**The label describes a shareholding pattern, not a verdict.** The [Department of Business Development](https://www.dbd.go.th) defines a nominee-risk entity as one with foreign shareholding of 0.01% to 49.99%, a band drawn for closer review, not a finding of illegality.[^2] Minority foreign shareholding is common and lawful.

The stock tells a different story from the flow. As at 21 June 2026, 119,297 companies sat within that band, against a total register of 1,002,685 entities.[^2] The band has built for decades, from 523 in 1998 to 11,746 in 2025.[^2] A gateway closing in August only slows how fast that total grows, and the same population anchors duties under the [Anti-Money Laundering Act](/resources/anti-money-laundering-act-thailand).

## Where Is Enforcement Actually Pointed?

**The ministry has already sent the existing stock somewhere.** Of the companies inside the screening category, 17,556 have gone to the [Land Department](https://www.dol.go.th) for property-holding checks and 14,800 to the Revenue Department for tax audit, drawn from the accumulated pool, not August's filings.[^2]

Field inspections between October 2025 and June 2026 covered 35 areas across 11 provinces, naming villas, construction, brokerage, tourism and law offices as target sectors.[^2] Phuket and Surat Thani, growth provinces in our [property market analysis](/resources/thailand-property-market-analysis), sit under the heaviest inspection too.

The minister also told Posttoday that over 50,000 high-risk records have gone to partner agencies for retrospective checks.[^1]

## What This Means If You're Already in a High-Risk Property-Owning Company

**A falling registration count changes nothing about your position.** If your company already sits inside the screening category, you are counted in the pool of 119,297, not the new-registration series the 75% describes; the referral figures describe where you stand.[^2]

That is the pattern behind every [high-risk property-owning company](/resources/nominee-company-risks-thailand). The [Foreign Business Act's](/resources/foreign-business-act-thailand) nominee offence, Section 36, reaches the Thai nominee and foreign beneficiary alike: up to three years and THB 100,000 to 1,000,000, courts able to order the shareholding to cease; the Land Department can force disposal separately. Directors carry exposure under [AMLA](https://www.amlo.go.th) section 60, one to ten years plus THB 20,000 to 200,000.
