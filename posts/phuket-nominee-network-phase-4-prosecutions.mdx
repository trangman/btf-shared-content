## What the Land Office Found

The land strand runs deeper than the raids. After a 25 July confrontation at Phuket's Chabad House, Deputy Interior Minister Polpee Suwanchawee widened the inspection programme: "This is Thailand. Everyone on Thai soil must comply with Thai law."[^3]

That is now the Phuket Provincial [Land Office](https://www.dol.go.th) and Thalang branch examining 361 companies for land held through foreign-majority shareholding: 114 sued, 39 fined, and 15 rai and 13 square wah of land sold, roughly 24,050 square metres.[^1]

A further 149 entities own land with over half foreign shareholding; disposal is under way there too. Phase 4 identified eight suspected nominee companies, taking such prosecuted cases to 31.[^1]

## What the New Registration Checks Now Test

New checks apply to any company with foreign investment, testing capital at formation, not years later at a raid.[^1] Registrars examine three months of bank statements against declared share values, verify the source of funds, and confirm business premises.

[Accountants and lawyers](/resources/gatekeeper-liability-definition-thailand) fall inside the perimeter too, verifying a company's identity through the registration system before filing.

A source-of-funds record is harder to coach than a witness. It sits with the [Department of Business Development](https://www.dbd.go.th) and [Ministry of Commerce](https://www.moc.go.th) before a share transfers: new foreign-invested registrations fell 57.64%, liquidations rose 20.65%, a [market signal](/resources/thailand-property-market-analysis) too.[^1]

## What This Means for Buyers Weighing a Structure

A Thai company is no shortcut anymore. The new checks, not a broker's promise, are the real test: bank statements and a source-of-funds review, now required before any land registers.

A compliant long-lease structure is judged on its own documents.
